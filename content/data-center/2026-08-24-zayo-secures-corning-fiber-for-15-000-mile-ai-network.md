Zayo has reserved manufacturing capacity with Corning for a material portion of the fiber it needs to build 15,000 route miles of new and overbuilt infrastructure by 2030, according to a report in [Data Center Knowledge](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/networking/zayo-locks-up-corning-fiber-capacity-for-ai-network-buildout). The agreement gives the telecommunications firm supply visibility for its AI-driven network expansion as demand for data center interconnect grows.

The expansion covers 28 routes, including 10 new builds and 18 overbuilds, totaling 20 million fiber miles. Zayo Chief Network Officer Troy Lupe told the publication that while the company has not faced material fiber shortages, it sees concerning signals in the supply chain. Manufacturing fiber requires long lead times for factory capacity, and the agreement lets Corning plan production lines around Zayo’s projected needs.

The secured capacity supports Zayo’s broader funded network buildout, which includes a partnership with Nvidia covering 24 of the 28 routes. That program accounts for 8,000 route miles and 15 million fiber miles. Zayo has delivered four routes in 2026 and expects four more by year-end, with most construction spanning the next several years. Permitting and construction across multiple jurisdictions pose the biggest execution risks, Lupe said.

AI workloads are changing fiber requirements. Dell’Oro Group vice president Jimmy Yu said AI networks need hundreds or thousands of fibers per conduit, far more than traditional networks. They also require physically diverse paths for resiliency, direct routes to reduce latency, and fewer add/drop points. HyperFrame Research vice president Ron Westfall called high-count fiber cabling a secondary structural constraint alongside power and other data center infrastructure.

The market reflects this shift. Optical transport equipment revenue grew 15 percent year over year in the second quarter, while data center interconnect revenue from Wavelength Division Multiplexing (WDM) systems and IP over Dense WDM (IPoDWDM) optics jumped 45 percent, according to Dell’Oro. Corning’s Optical Communications business grew 36 percent year over year in the first quarter, driven by AI network builds.

For network operators, the agreement demonstrates a proactive approach to supply chain risk. Securing fiber capacity years ahead reduces uncertainty for long-haul projects that require extensive engineering and permitting. It also signals that fiber availability is becoming a strategic bottleneck in AI infrastructure planning, requiring operators to coordinate earlier with manufacturers and lock in capacity before demand intensifies.
