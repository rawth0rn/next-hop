TE Connectivity, in partnership with NVIDIA and Cisco, announced a new standard for 448G front-panel connectivity at the [Open Compute Project (OCP) Global Summit 2026](https://www.servethehome.com/te-connectivity-at-ocp-summit-2026-the-interview/). The announcement centers on a new module form factor that addresses the signal integrity and thermal challenges inherent to 448G Serializer/Deserializer (SerDes) speeds while maintaining dimensions comparable to existing Quad Small Form-factor Pluggable (QSFP) modules.

The standard introduces a mechanical separation between power delivery and signal integrity paths. Brian Payne, Vice President of Product Management at TE Connectivity, explained that the transceiver slides into a housing that compresses to isolate these functions. This separation allows engineers to optimize signal integrity without compromising power delivery capacity, and vice versa. The design also incorporates provisions for cold plate liquid cooling to manage escalating transceiver thermal densities, a feature that becomes necessary as port power climbs with each speed generation.

For data center operators, the specification provides a concrete mechanical target for future front-panel interconnects. The 448G SerDes rate (448 Gbps per lane) pushes copper connectivity toward its physical limits, requiring careful attention to picojoules-per-bit efficiency and trace lengths. By preserving a QSFP-like form factor, the standard offers a potential migration path that protects existing rack real estate and cabling infrastructure investments. The collaboration between TE Connectivity, NVIDIA, and Cisco signals alignment among switch silicon vendors, interconnect manufacturers, and systems integrators on the physical layer requirements necessary to scale AI clusters beyond current 800G and 1.6T aggregate speeds.

The announcement arrives as the industry debates the transition point from copper to co-packaged optics. TE Connectivity noted that copper remains the state of the art for front-panel connectivity, and this standard extends its viability for rack-scale deployments while the industry continues to invest in optical alternatives.
