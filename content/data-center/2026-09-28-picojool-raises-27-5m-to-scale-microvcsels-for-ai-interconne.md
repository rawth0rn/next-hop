PicoJool raised a $27.5 million Series A round to scale production of microVCSEL optical modules for AI data center interconnects, according to [Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/pat-gelsinger-backed-optical-startup-raises-275m-to-scale-vcsels-for-ai-links/).

The Palo Alto startup builds vertical-cavity surface-emitting laser (VCSEL) chips and modules for dense optical links inside AI clusters. Its microVCSEL products shrink the laser footprint and cut power compared with conventional wire-bonded VCSELs. The company has already demonstrated 200G per lane and is targeting scale-up links that support more than 37 gigahertz of bandwidth.

PicoJool fabricates its devices on gallium arsenide (GaAs) to avoid the supply constraints that plague some alternative optical technologies. It is working with foundries including WIN Semiconductor to qualify chips and microVCSEL components for production. The funding will expand facilities in the US and Taiwan and let the company move from discrete components to complete active optical cable (AOC) and near-packaged optics (NPO) modules.

The round was led by Socratic Partners, the firm run by former NXP Semiconductor CEO Rick Clemmer, with participation from Hudson River Trading. That brings total funding to $39.5 million after a seed round led by former Intel CEO Pat Gelsinger’s Playground Global.

For operators building AI infrastructure, the pitch is straightforward: lower-power lasers that can be packed more tightly mean less heat and higher front-panel density. The GaAs supply chain is mature and uncrowded, which reduces procurement risk compared with newer co-packaged optics approaches. CEO Al Yuen, who led VCSEL development at Lumentum for more than a decade, said the focus is now on execution and customer qualification.
