Cisco shipped $4 billion in artificial intelligence systems and components to hyperscalers during its fourth fiscal quarter of 2026, a nearly fivefold increase year over year, according to [The Next Platform](https://www.nextplatform.com/connect/2026/08/17/cisco-can-finally-sell-lots-of-supercomputers-and-their-networks/5288804). The company is selling converged switch-systems built around its Silicon One application-specific integrated circuits (ASICs) and Acacia optical transceivers to customers including neoclouds, sovereigns, and large enterprises that already buy its Unified Computing System (UCS) platforms.

Cisco entered the converged switch-system business in 2009 with the launch of its UCS platform. The company acquired Acacia for $4.5 billion in 2021 to address shortages of 400 Gb/s and 800 Gb/s optical transceivers. The Silicon One architecture converges switching and routing into a single design, with tunable variants for scale-up networking to share memory across GPUs and accelerators in AI clusters. The ASICs allow for tuning specific to AI workloads as well as variants for scale-up networking. During the quarter, Cisco shipped 100,000 400 Gb/s transceivers and 35,000 800 Gb/s transceivers, bringing cumulative totals to 850,000 and 75,000 units respectively. The company also recorded $475 million in orders from neoclouds and sovereigns for AI components and systems.

The systems compete directly with offerings from Hewlett Packard Enterprise, Dell, and Lenovo in the high-performance computing market. Unlike original design manufacturers, Cisco sells its own chippery, systems, and software rather than simply integrating third-party components. This allows the company to capture margin on ASICs and optics rather than just server assembly.

For enterprises already running Cisco UCS infrastructure, the vendor provides an upgrade path for agentic AI workloads that builds on existing campus and datacenter switching deployments. Modern AI rackscale systems bear resemblance to converged UCS chassis from prior generations, though with denser compute and higher power requirements. Cisco expects fiscal 2027 revenue between $72.2 billion and $73.4 billion.
