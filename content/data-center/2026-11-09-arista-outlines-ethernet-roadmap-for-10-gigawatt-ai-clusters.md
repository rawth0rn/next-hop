Arista Networks outlined plans to supply switching fabrics for 10 gigawatts of AI cluster capacity by the end of 2027, according to chief executive officer Jayshree Ullal on the company's third quarter financial call [The Next Platform](https://www.nextplatform.com/connect/2026/11/05/arista-lines-up-networking-for-10-gigawatts-of-ai-systems/5307929).

The roadmap centers on the ESUN protocol for scale up networking, connecting accelerators within racks. Arista expects trials to begin in 2027 with rackscale systems supporting 128 to 144 accelerators per rack and as many as 1,000 accelerators across a row. Production rollouts for these back end fabrics will not arrive until 2028. Until then, Arista continues to ship front end Ethernet switches that link AI clusters to storage and external networks, plus R series switches that provide routing functions to scale across datacenter boundaries.

Ullal stated that Arista worked with AMD, Arm, Meta Platforms, Microsoft, and Qualcomm on rackscale switching based on its Etherlink product lines. Microsoft and Meta, which each account for 10 percent or more of Arista revenue, are developing ESUN networks for their respective Maia and MTIA accelerators. By 2027, Arista expects to support ten or more accelerator types, diversifying beyond Nvidia's proprietary NVLink fabrics.

The 10 gigawatt target represents a significant scaling challenge. AI training keeps accelerators busy waiting for data, so idle time is economically devastating. Arista addresses this by embedding AI into its Autonomous Virtual Assistant management tools to process telemetry at machine speed, a task human operators cannot perform. The scale up fabrics will coexist with scale across networks that connect clusters spanning multiple datacenters, necessary because individual facilities face power constraints.

For network operators, the timeline matters. ESUN trials begin next year, but production back end Ethernet for AI remains two years out. Current AI revenue comes from front end and scale across deployments, which Arista forecasts will reach $3.5 billion in 2026 and double to $7 billion in 2027. Practitioners planning new AI clusters should note that multi-vendor accelerator support via Ethernet will not mature before 2028.
