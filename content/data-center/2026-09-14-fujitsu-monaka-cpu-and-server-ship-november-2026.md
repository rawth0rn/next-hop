Fujitsu will make its Monaka central processing unit (CPU) available for purchase starting November 2026, with rack-mounted servers containing the chip shipping to select customers in financial, telecommunications, and manufacturing sectors in the second half of 2026 and broader shipments to Japan and Europe commencing April 2027, the company [announced](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/fujitsu-announces-monaka-cpu-and-server-will-be-available-from-november/).

The Fujitsu-Monaka CPU is built on 2nm technology and uses a microarchitecture optimized for advanced 3D packaging and ultra-low voltage circuit operation. The chip runs at a maximum frequency of 3.8GHz and supports memory transfer speeds of 8800 megatransfers per second (MTps). Fujitsu designed the processor to integrate with GPUs and other accelerators, positioning it specifically for AI inference and AI agent workloads in enterprise data centers that require high throughput with reduced power draw.

The server hardware comes in 1U and 2U rack-mounted form factors. The 1U variant accommodates one or two CPUs, while the 2U variant holds two CPUs. According to Fujitsu, the servers can operate in ambient temperatures up to 40 degrees Celsius with air cooling, or water temperatures up to 45 degrees Celsius with water cooling, without requiring specialized cooling infrastructure. This thermal tolerance allows deployment in standard air-cooled data centers that lack dedicated liquid cooling systems, reducing both infrastructure requirements and operational complexity for facility managers.

Fujitsu states the CPU delivers twice the AI inference throughput compared to other CPUs, which the company says allows operators to halve the server count and power consumption for equivalent processing loads. The company also claims the design reduces server cooling power consumption by up to 80 percent when operating in air-cooled environments. The Monaka server is developed and manufactured entirely in Japan, offering regional supply chain assurance for domestic operators in financial services, telecommunications, and manufacturing industries who prioritize local sourcing and security.

Select customers will receive systems in the second half of 2026. Broader shipments to Japan and Europe start April 2027. Fujitsu plans to expand the lineup to include high-density servers for AI data centers and rack-scale servers that enable autonomous operation, including automated maintenance using robots.
