Oracle has expanded its 2026 restructuring plan, adding $700 million in expected costs to bring the total to $2.8 billion, according to a quarterly filing reported by [Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/oracle-upscales-layoffs-expected-to-increase-restructuring-costs-by-700m/). The company first announced the plan at the start of 2026 with an estimated cost of $2.1 billion.

The increase reflects additional layoffs beyond the thousands of job cuts already implemented since the plan began in April 2026. The company's employee count dropped to 141,000 as of May 31, 2026, down from 162,000 the previous year. The 21,000 job cuts over the 12-month period resulted in $1.84 billion in severance payments and exit costs.

Oracle's latest quarterly filing states that management decided to supplement the plan "by approximately $700 million to reflect additional actions that we expect to take." The filing indicates these costs are "primarily related to employee severance costs," signaling further workforce reductions.

The restructuring comes despite strong financial results. Oracle reported Infrastructure as a Service revenue up 121 percent year-over-year to $7.4 billion for its first quarter of 2027. Remaining performance obligations grew $209 billion year-over-year to $664 billion.

In a separate move, Oracle chairman Larry Ellison canceled plans to sell up to 50 million shares of Oracle common stock one day after announcing them. No explanation was provided, and the company stated Ellison "has no other plans to sell any of his Oracle stock."

The expanded restructuring suggests Oracle is continuing to streamline operations even as its cloud infrastructure business grows rapidly. For network operators and data center practitioners, the cuts may affect Oracle Cloud's support and service delivery, though the company has not detailed which divisions face the latest round of reductions. The timing coincides with Oracle's major IaaS revenue growth, creating uncertainty about how the reduced workforce will support expanding cloud infrastructure.
