LatConnect 60 has signed a commercial contract with [Transcelestial](https://spacenews.com/latconnect-60-selects-transcelestials-space-optical-network-for-swirsat-imaging-constellation/) for an optical communications terminal on its SWIRSAT-1 mission, the companies announced.

The agreement covers a terminal designed for rates up to 10Gbps and access to Transcelestial’s optical ground station network, which has two stations live today in Singapore and Spain and targets five to six by the end of 2026. A Transcelestial terminal is already in orbit on the 6G StarLab mission. LatConnect 60 (LC60) will use the link to move Short Wave Infra Red (SWIR) imagery from its spacecraft into its processing environment. The deal represents the second Australian mission to adopt Transcelestial’s optical downlink, following integration with Gilmour Space.

Typical small-satellite X-band links run at 100 Mbps, moving roughly 5GB during a seven-minute pass. The same pass carries roughly 50GB at 1Gbps or 500GB at 10Gbps on the optical service. LC60 is procuring the flight terminal and ground connectivity as a single managed service rather than building its own optical ground segment or securing radio frequency spectrum licences. The transport layer provides equivalent capacity to dark fibre from satellite to ground without the spectrum licensing that radio frequency downlink requires.

The arrangement lets LC60 retain mission tasking, data ownership, and customer-delivery control while Transcelestial operates the transport layer. The narrow optical beam is harder to intercept than radio frequency signals, requiring physical presence in the beam path, and the link is not exposed to broadband jamming. Transcelestial has operated post-quantum cryptography in production since March 2026 to add quantum-resistant protection at the application layer.

Network diversity supports availability. With optical ground stations in multiple locations, weather that closes one station routes the downlink to another. Adaptive data rates and forward error correction raise throughput continuously as the geometry improves within a pass.

LC60 plans to launch two SWIRSAT satellites in the first quarter of 2027 and scale to an 18-satellite constellation by 2029. Transcelestial is scaling production to more than 100 optical terminals per month from its own facility.
