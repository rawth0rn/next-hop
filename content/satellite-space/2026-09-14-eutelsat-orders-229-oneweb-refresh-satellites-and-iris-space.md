Eutelsat has authorized Airbus Defence and Space to proceed with building 229 additional OneWeb satellites and selected Aerospacelab as prime contractor for 264 IRIS² satellite platforms, marking a major consolidation of Europe’s low Earth orbit (LEO) manufacturing capacity, according to [Via Satellite](https://www.satellitetoday.com/manufacturing/2026/09/10/eutelsat-taps-airbus-and-aerospacelab-for-oneweb-and-iris%c2%b2-satellites/).

The OneWeb refresh program aims to maintain service continuity for the constellation completed in 2023. The first satellites from Airbus’s existing order are due for delivery next quarter, with two dedicated Ariane 64 launches secured for 2027 and 2028. Eutelsat is investing an estimated 1 billion euro ($1.16 billion) in the additional satellites, which will feature advanced digital channelizers for onboard processing and hosted payload capabilities. Airbus is building the refresh satellites at its Toulouse facility, a shift from the first-generation production in Florida.

For the IRIS² program, Aerospacelab’s 2.4 billion euro ($2.8 billion) contract covers 264 dual Ku/Ka-band satellite platforms. The Belgium-based manufacturer, founded in 2018, will produce the spacecraft at its new Megafactory. Airbus will separately build 66 LEO Ka-band satellites for IRIS², targeted for delivery in 2029. Thales Alenia Space will supply payloads for all 330 IRIS² satellites under a 500 million euro ($581 million) order.

IRIS² is planned as Europe’s third flagship space program after Galileo and Copernicus, with 348 satellites across MEO and LEO to provide secure connectivity for EU member states. The SpaceRISE consortium, comprising SES, Eutelsat, and Hispasat, is leading development. SES recently ordered 18 MEO satellites from OHB for nearly 1 billion euro ($1.16 billion) to form the MEO component.

The industrial split reflects a deliberate strategy to scale European manufacturing. Aerospacelab CEO Benoît Deper called the selection a defining moment, noting institutions are trusting a new generation of manufacturers built for speed and scale. For network operators, the procurement signals a clear timeline for European sovereign broadband capacity, with the first IRIS² deployments arriving in 2029 and OneWeb refresh satellites launching within months.
