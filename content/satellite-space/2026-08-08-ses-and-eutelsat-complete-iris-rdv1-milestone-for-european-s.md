SES and Eutelsat completed the Rendez-vous 1 (RDV1) milestone for the Infrastructure for Resilience, Interconnectivity and Security by Satellite (IRIS²) program, moving the European Union’s sovereign constellation from design into implementation. [Via Satellite](https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2026/08/07/ses-eutelsat-complete-rdv1-milestone-for-europes-iris2-sovereign-constellation-program/)

The RDV1 milestone triggers the concession contract signed in December 2024 between the European Commission, the European Space Agency (ESA), and the SpaceRISE consortium led by SES. Under the agreement, SES will design, build, and test the payloads for 18 Medium Earth Orbit (MEO) satellites, with service entry targeted for 2030. Eutelsat also completed its RDV1 milestone on Friday. The complete constellation aims to provide secure, resilient connectivity for European government and commercial users, filling gaps in existing coverage.

SES committed up to €1.35 billion for its MEO segment and stated it does not expect that figure to rise further to fund the project. When operational, SES will control approximately 90 percent of the MEO capacity for commercial sale across government, mobility, enterprise, and cloud markets. The operator will also lead user terminal development and run combined Low Earth Orbit (LEO) and MEO service operations through IRIS² control centers.

For network operators, IRIS² offers a government-backed alternative to purely commercial satellite services, with fixed capital commitments and defined timelines. The 2030 target gives enterprises a concrete date for planning sovereign connectivity upgrades, while the split between LEO and MEO segments provides options for latency-sensitive and high-throughput applications. Completion of RDV1 signals that procurement and construction are proceeding on schedule, reducing uncertainty for customers waiting for the service to come online.
