Himera and GomSpace announced Oct. 27 that their Obria Nano satellite, launched Oct. 1 aboard the SpaceX Transporter-18 rideshare mission, is undergoing tests in orbit ([SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/ukrainian-startup-gomspace-test-satellite-for-future-communications-network/)). The Ukrainian defense technology startup and the Danish satellite manufacturer plan to use the results to design a constellation that would provide communications coverage across Ukraine.

The companies did not disclose the satellite's specifications, communications capacity, or project financing. GomSpace, headquartered in Aalborg, Denmark, manufactures small satellites, communications payloads, and spacecraft subsystems. Himera, founded in 2022, develops secure tactical radio systems for military forces, including equipment designed to operate in environments affected by electronic warfare. The firms stated that Obria Nano is intended to establish a communications system under Ukrainian control, with plans eventually to manufacture satellites and ground terminals domestically. Himera co-founder and chief executive Misha Rudominski said the project aims to lay the foundation for an independent communications network while developing capabilities that strengthen the country's strategic security.

The project follows disruptions in mid-October to SpaceX's Starlink and Starshield services that interfered with Ukrainian drone operations and military command networks. Ukraine has relied on these commercial services since 2022 for battlefield communications in areas where terrestrial infrastructure has been destroyed. Restrictions imposed during the incident limited satellite connectivity and affected efforts to intercept Russian attack drones. While Starlink and Starshield services returned to normal operating speeds by Oct. 24, the incident highlighted operational risks of dependence on external providers.

A sovereign constellation would not replace Starlink's extensive global network. Instead, smaller national constellations could provide dedicated communications for selected military operations while supplementing commercial services. The current testing phase will inform the design and scale of the proposed network, though the firms did not specify the number of satellites envisioned or a timeline for deployment.
