Astrolight has signed a memorandum of understanding with ATMOS Space Cargo to demonstrate an in-flight optical communications link between a reentry vehicle and an orbiting satellite in 2027, according to [SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/astrolight-atmos-space-cargo-eye-first-in-flight-laser-link-between-reentry-vehicle-satellite/). The demonstration would mark the first time such a link has been attempted outside ground-based laboratory conditions.

During the mission, Astrolight’s ATLAS-X laser communication terminals will fly onboard ATMOS’ PHOENIX reentry vehicle and the host satellite. The terminals will establish an optical link to transmit system and mission data at rates up to 2.5 Gbps during both orbital operations and atmospheric reentry. This differs from traditional radio frequency (RF) communications, which suffer from limited bandwidth and higher susceptibility to interference.

Laser links rely on narrow, focused beams that are significantly harder to jam, intercept, or detect than RF signals. The companies state that laser communications can achieve data rates up to 100 times higher than conventional RF systems. For reentry vehicles, which typically experience communications blackouts during descent, maintaining a high-bandwidth connection with an orbiting satellite provides continuous telemetry that ground stations cannot offer during the critical reentry phase.

The demonstration supports broader efforts to expand European cargo-return capabilities. Europe currently relies on international partners to transport cargo to and from Low Earth Orbit (LEO). The European Space Agency (ESA) LEO Cargo Return Services Initiative aims to reduce this dependency by developing local return services. Real-time connectivity via satellite laser links would allow operators to monitor and control autonomous reentry missions without waiting for ground station passes, making operations more efficient and scalable.

If the 2027 test succeeds, it could establish a template for connecting reentry vehicles directly to satellite constellations, enabling high-data-rate communications for commercial logistics and defense missions.
