The European Commission has finalized an implementation agreement with the SpaceRISE consortium to expand the IRIS² secure connectivity constellation, adding 66 satellites and raising the total cost to 15.6 billion euros [SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/europe-increases-size-and-cost-of-iris%c2%b2-constellation/).

IRIS² is Europe's sovereign satellite network for government and defense communications. The original plan called for 18 satellites in medium Earth orbit (MEO) and 264 in high low Earth orbit (high LEO) at 1,200 kilometers, plus a handful of lower LEO satellites for technology incubation. The revised architecture keeps the 18 MEO satellites and adds 66 more to the high LEO shell, bringing that total to 330. The commission says this boosts secure governmental capacity by 60 percent within the European Union (EU) and 54 percent outside it. Hispasat confirmed it remains lead operator for the lower LEO technology incubation layer.

The expansion signals Europe's commitment to owning its satellite infrastructure rather than relying on commercial operators or foreign systems. Launch timing stays at 2029, so the additional satellites must fit the same deployment schedule. The cost jumped 47 percent from the 10.6 billion euro estimate when SpaceRISE won the contract in December 2024, and it is now 160 percent higher than the original 6 billion euro proposal. The SpaceRISE consortium, led by Eutelsat, Hispasat, and SES, will cover 4 billion euros. The European Commission and European Space Agency (ESA) will supply the remaining 11.6 billion euros through the 2028-2034 budget cycle and member state contributions. Spain pledged 1.6 to 2 billion euros, and Poland committed about 750 million dollars for six MEO and six LEO satellites. For network operators, this means a guaranteed, EU-backed satellite service with dedicated capacity for defense and security traffic, but also a longer timeline before operational availability and a larger public funding burden.

The final agreement, known as Rendezvous 1, locks in the design and clears the way for production. The commission now must secure the additional budget through its next multiannual financial framework.
