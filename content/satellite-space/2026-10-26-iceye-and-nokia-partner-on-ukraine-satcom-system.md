Iceye and Nokia signed a letter of intent with the Ministry of Defence of Ukraine to develop a satellite communications capability for the country, [Via Satellite reported](https://www.satellitetoday.com/government-military/2026/10/22/iceye-and-nokia-look-to-develop-satcom-capability-for-ukraine/). The agreement focuses on building a sovereign satcom system that Ukraine would own and control.

The partners said the system would be built on trusted European technology. Iceye, a synthetic aperture radar (SAR) provider, is expanding into satcom through the effort. Nokia is supplying its telecom infrastructure expertise. The companies did not disclose technical specifications, orbital architecture, or a delivery schedule.

This marks a shift for Iceye. The firm has provided SAR imaging to Ukraine since 2022, but this is its first move into communications satellites, ground segment, and terminals. Nokia's role centers on integrating the communications payload and network elements into a deployable system. The announcement was light on details, though the partners emphasized national ownership of the full stack.

For network operators and government customers, the deal highlights the operational risk of relying on commercial satcom that can be throttled or restricted by a foreign vendor. Ukraine recently reported that access to SpaceX's Starshield variant of Starlink was slowed on moving platforms, which impacted drone guidance and air defense. SpaceX CEO Elon Musk said any decision to restrict Starshield would come from the U.S. government, but the Wall Street Journal reported that Musk himself ordered the slowdown. The incident has intensified European calls to reduce dependence on Starlink.

A sovereign system would give Ukraine direct control over its links. That matters for contested environments where guaranteed bandwidth and command authority are operational necessities rather than conveniences. For practitioners, the Iceye and Nokia effort is a signal that European nations are prioritizing owned infrastructure over leased commercial capacity, even if the timelines and budgets remain undefined.
