[Iridium](https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2026/09/11/iridium-sends-voice-message-from-toyota-vehicle-with-deutsche-telekom-iot/) transmitted a voice message from a moving Toyota vehicle using its satellite-based NTN Direct service and Deutsche Telekom IoT, demonstrating standards-based narrowband Internet of Things (NB-IoT) connectivity outside terrestrial coverage.

The demonstration used a Nordic Semiconductor nRF9151 development board and a Deutsche Telekom IoT Subscriber Identity Module installed in the vehicle. A Fraunhofer voice codec compressed the audio data for transmission over Iridium’s Low-Earth Orbit (LEO) constellation. The message traveled from the vehicle to the satellite constellation, then downlinked to Iridium’s ground infrastructure, where it integrated with Deutsche Telekom’s global Internet of Things backbone.

The test relied on Iridium NTN Direct, a non-terrestrial network service announced in 2024. Iridium plans to make the service commercially available in the fourth quarter of this year. The platform allows NB-IoT devices to roam between cellular towers and satellite links without hardware modifications, using standard 3rd Generation Partnership Project (3GPP) radio protocols and a single SIM profile. Selected European customers are already testing applications on the Iridium network using Deutsche Telekom’s global SIM.

For network operators and systems architects, the demonstration shows how 3GPP Release 17 satellite connectivity can extend machine-to-machine communications beyond cellular footprints without proprietary radios or custom firmware. The Fraunhofer codec addresses the bandwidth constraints typical of satellite links, making voice practical over narrowband channels previously limited to small data bursts. Automotive fleets, logistics trackers, remote utility sensors, and agricultural monitors can use existing NB-IoT modems to report status or send emergency alerts from anywhere under Iridium’s polar-orbit coverage.

The integration with Deutsche Telekom IoT means enterprises can manage satellite and terrestrial subscriptions through a single SIM contract, simplifying logistics for global deployments and reducing the operational overhead of multi-vendor connectivity contracts. As the service moves toward commercial launch later this year, practitioners should evaluate whether standards-based satellite backhaul fits their rural or mobile use cases, and whether current device power budgets can support the higher link margins required for satellite transmission.
