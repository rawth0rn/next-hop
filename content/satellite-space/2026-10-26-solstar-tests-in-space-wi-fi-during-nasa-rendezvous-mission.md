Solstar Space demonstrated in-space Wi-Fi connectivity between two orbiting spacecraft during a recent rendezvous and proximity operations (RPO) mission with NASA [Via Satellite](https://www.satellitetoday.com/space-economy/2026/10/23/solstar-demonstrated-space-wi-fi-technology-in-recent-rpo-mission-with-nasa/). The company’s Deke Space Communicator, riding as a hosted payload on the Momentus Vigoride-7 orbital service vehicle, established a wireless link with the NASA R5-S10 cubesat while both vehicles flew in formation. The cubesat captured imagery of the orbital service vehicle to assess its health and performance during the proximity maneuver, demonstrating how standard wireless protocols can function in the space environment.

During the 30-minute proximity window, the Deke payload collected more than 2,300 files from the cubesat using Wi-Fi transmission in space. The dataset included imagery alongside optical, infrared, and Light Detection and Ranging (LiDAR) sensor readings. Rather than storing data onboard for later downlink, the communicator relayed this information in near real-time to NASA Johnson Space Center through experimental use of the Iridium satellite network. This created a complete space-to-space-to-ground data chain without requiring the cubesat to maintain direct radio contact with ground stations during the critical maneuver, bypassing the coverage limitations that typically constrain small satellite operations.

For satellite operators and mission controllers, the demonstration validates near real-time networking during close-proximity operations. RPO missions demand continuous health monitoring and precise coordination between vehicles. Wi-Fi links allow spacecraft to exchange telemetry and sensor data immediately while maintaining formation, eliminating the delays and coverage gaps inherent in store-and-forward architectures that rely on intermittent ground station passes. This capability becomes critical for future on-orbit servicing, assembly, and manufacturing missions where multiple vehicles must operate as coordinated systems and share situational awareness without waiting for ground station access.

By using existing commercial satellite infrastructure for the backhaul link rather than building dedicated relay networks, operators can reduce both cost and operational complexity. The approach allows small cubesats with limited power budgets to offload data immediately to a nearby service vehicle, which then handles the long-haul transmission to Earth. This architecture extends mission connectivity windows and ensures that critical telemetry reaches flight controllers during time-sensitive proximity operations when vehicle safety depends on rapid data availability.
