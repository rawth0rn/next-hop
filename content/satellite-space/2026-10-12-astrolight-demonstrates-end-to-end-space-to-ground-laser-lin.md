Astrolight [established](https://www.satellitetoday.com/technology/2026/10/09/astrolight-delivers-space-to-ground-laser-communications-links/) end-to-end optical communication links between its ATLAS-1 laser communication terminal in orbit and its OGS-2.5 optical ground station in Greece. The company announced on October 8 that the link moved from first light to routine operations at 1 Gbps within one week.

The ATLAS-1 terminal flies aboard the ERMIS-3 nanosatellite. It connected with the HolOGS-1 optical ground station in Holomondas, Greece, which Astrolight upgraded to the OGS-2.5 model for this mission. The demonstration achieved first light during the satellite's first active pass over the station. By the third pass, both systems operated autonomously on a pre-scheduled basis at each end of the link. Astrolight's team in Lithuania operated both the space and ground systems remotely without requiring local personnel at the ground station.

The terminal currently transmits data at 1 Gbps. Link budget margins indicate the system could support rates of up to 10 Gbps. Astrolight developed the terminal working with the European Space Agency and collaborated with the Department of Aerospace of the University of Athens and the Department of Physics of the Aristotle University of Thessaloniki on the project. The company developed the OGS-2.5 ground station specifically for the PeakSat mission.

For satellite operators, the milestone matters because it demonstrates that high-capacity optical links can move from experimental first contact to routine autonomous operations within days rather than months. The ability to deploy and operate these systems remotely with minimal oversight reduces operational costs and staffing requirements for commercial missions. Proving reliable space-to-ground laser connectivity at gigabit speeds brings optical communications closer to practical deployment at scale for earth observation and data relay constellations. This operational model addresses the traditional barrier to laser communications adoption, which has been the complexity of maintaining precise pointing acquisition and tracking between orbiting assets and ground stations.
