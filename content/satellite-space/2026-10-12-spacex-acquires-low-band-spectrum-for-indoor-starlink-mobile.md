SpaceX will acquire nationwide low-band spectrum from Grain Management to expand Starlink Mobile coverage indoors [Via Satellite](https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2026/10/08/spacex-moves-to-acquire-nationwide-low-band-spectrum-for-starlink-mobile/). The deal, announced Thursday, gives SpaceX radio frequencies that penetrate buildings better than the mid-band spectrum Starlink uses today.

Low-band spectrum typically spans frequencies below 1 GHz. Signals at these wavelengths travel farther and pass through obstacles like walls and windows with less attenuation than higher frequencies. For satellite services, this means a handset can maintain a link without a direct line of sight to the sky. The trade-off is lower bandwidth per megahertz, but nationwide low-band holdings provide the foundation for basic mobile service in areas terrestrial networks do not reach.

For network operators and mobile planners, this acquisition signals a shift in satellite coverage models. Indoor service has been the boundary where satellite phones failed. Starlink Mobile with low-band spectrum could serve as a backup for public safety, fill coverage gaps in rural deployments, and provide baseline connectivity for Internet of Things devices outside terrestrial range. The move also sets up potential roaming agreements, since the spectrum aligns with bands terrestrial carriers already use.

Practically, this means engineers designing hybrid networks can now consider satellite as a viable indoor fallback. The link budget improves enough that standard handsets, not just specialized satellite phones, might maintain service through a single exterior wall. Network planners should watch for SpaceX’s coverage maps and handset compatibility lists, as the low-band acquisition creates new options for mission-critical communication architectures.

The deal’s terms remain undisclosed, and the timeline for service launch is not public. What is clear is that SpaceX is building the spectrum portfolio needed to compete with traditional mobile operators on their own terms: coverage everywhere, including inside buildings.
