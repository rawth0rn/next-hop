Amazon lost its first batch of Project Kuiper broadband satellites after a United Launch Alliance (ULA) Vulcan rocket suffered an in-flight anomaly during ascent, according to [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/networks/2026/11/16/amazon-loses-first-batch-of-broadband-satellites-launched-on-custom-rocket/5310000).

The satellites were bound for Low Earth Orbit (LEO) to join Amazon's planned constellation of over 3,000 spacecraft. These units carry Ka-band payloads designed to deliver broadband internet service to unserved regions. The launch used a custom configuration of the Vulcan rocket, which United Launch Alliance (ULA) developed to replace its Atlas V and Delta IV fleets. The anomaly occurred during the ascent phase, resulting in the complete loss of the payload. These spacecraft represented Amazon's first operational satellites following prototype launches, intended to establish the initial orbital shell and validate production designs for the full constellation.

This setback directly impacts Amazon's deployment timeline against regulatory mandates from the Federal Communications Commission (FCC). The company must place half of its planned constellation into orbit by July 2026 to maintain its spectrum licenses. With SpaceX's Starlink already operating thousands of satellites and offering commercial service, Amazon faces compressed schedules to achieve minimum viable coverage. The loss also affects United Launch Alliance's launch manifest reliability, as the provider had positioned Vulcan as its primary ride for heavy national security and commercial payloads through the remainder of the decade. Manufacturing replacement satellites requires production line capacity, integration schedules, and fresh launch windows, pushing revenue service availability further into 2027 or beyond. For network operators watching alternative last-mile options, this extends the timeline before Kuiper becomes a viable backup to terrestrial fiber and existing satellite services.

Amazon will coordinate with ULA on the anomaly investigation while accelerating satellite production to close the schedule gap.
