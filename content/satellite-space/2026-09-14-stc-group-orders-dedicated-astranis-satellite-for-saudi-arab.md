Stc Group ordered a dedicated satellite from Astranis to provide satellite communication services in Saudi Arabia, according to [Via Satellite](https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2026/09/12/stc-group-orders-dedicated-astranis-satellite-for-saudi-arabia/).

Astranis builds smaller Geostationary Orbit (GEO) satellites than traditional GEO satellites. The deal gives Stc Group, Saudi Arabia's top telecommunications provider, a dedicated spacecraft rather than shared capacity on a larger bus. Traditional GEO satellites often host multiple tenants with payloads sized for broad coverage areas spanning multiple countries or continents. A dedicated small GEO satellite allows a single operator to control the entire orbital asset without coordinating with other users or sharing power, bandwidth, and operational schedules. This single-tenant approach contrasts with the conventional model where national operators lease capacity from international satellite service providers.

For network operators, the dedicated model offers an alternative to leasing transponders on multi-user birds or building traditional GEO fleets. The smaller form factor suggests different mass and power constraints than conventional GEO programs, though specific weights, timelines, and throughput figures were not disclosed. For Stc Group, the arrangement means direct ownership of orbital capacity tailored to Saudi Arabian coverage requirements rather than purchasing capacity from a third-party operator. This direct ownership simplifies operations by eliminating coordination with other tenants and allows the operator to optimize the satellite's design for its specific coverage goals and service levels without compromise. The model also reduces dependency on external satellite operators for critical national infrastructure and may allow faster deployment than traditional GEO procurement cycles.

The order reflects growing interest among national telecoms in bespoke GEO assets that match national footprints without the excess capacity of traditional global beams. It signals that regional operators see custom small GEO payloads as viable infrastructure for national connectivity. It also positions Astranis as a vendor for telecoms seeking dedicated space infrastructure outside the shared-service model that dominates traditional GEO markets. The deal indicates that the market for national-scale satellite connectivity now includes options beyond large shared platforms.
