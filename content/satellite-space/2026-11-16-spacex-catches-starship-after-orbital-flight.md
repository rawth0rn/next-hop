SpaceX caught the Starship vehicle with launch tower arms after it completed an orbital flight on Saturday, marking the first successful recovery of the upper stage following a mission to orbit. [Via Satellite reported](https://www.satellitetoday.com/launch/2026/11/15/spacex-catches-starship-in-landing-after-orbital-flight-for-first-time/) that the catch occurred at 3:20 p.m. CST, one day after liftoff from Starbase, Texas.

The recovery used the Mechzilla launch tower's chopstick arms to grab the vehicle as it descended. Starship relit a single Raptor engine to perform a deorbit burn, then ignited three engines to slow its approach to the tower. This method differs from previous tests that ended in ocean splashdowns. While SpaceX has caught the Super Heavy booster on three prior flights, this marks the first time the company recovered the orbital vehicle itself.

During the flight, Starship spent more than 21 hours in orbit and deployed 26 satellites for the Starlink V3 broadband constellation. The mission demonstrates that the vehicle can now complete a full cycle: launch, orbital insertion, payload deployment, and return for reuse.

For network operators, full reusability changes the economics of deploying satellite broadband infrastructure. Catching both the booster and the orbital vehicle eliminates the cost of building new hardware for each launch. This allows SpaceX to launch Starlink V3 satellites at higher cadence, increasing constellation density and capacity. The Starlink V3 satellites provide the broadband backhaul that rural and mobile networks rely on, so faster deployment timelines directly affect ground network planning and capacity expansion schedules. Lower launch costs also put pressure on competing satellite operators to reduce their own deployment expenses or risk losing ground station and backhaul contracts.

The Super Heavy booster was not recovered during this flight due to an early termination of its boostback burn, which resulted in a splashdown in the Gulf. SpaceX will need to achieve a double-catch mission to realize fully reusable stack operations.
