The International Telecommunication Union Telecommunication Standardization Sector (ITU-T) [approved Recommendation Y.3335](https://www.lightreading.com/optical-networking/japan-secures-global-itu-t-standard-for-all-photonics-networks) on August 13, establishing a framework for all-photonics networks (APN) that can span multiple carriers and countries. Seven Japanese firms including KDDI, NTT, Rakuten Mobile, NEC, 1Finity, Oki Electric Industry, and Sumitomo Electric Industries developed the standard, which defines network configurations and technical requirements for setting up low-latency, energy-efficient communication paths between different operators.

APN technology uses light to transmit data at high speeds while significantly reducing energy consumption. The Y.3335 recommendation systematically organizes accumulated knowledge of APNs and research outcomes on photonic network federation technology, independent of specific organizations or implementation methods. This provides a common framework for operators to build services that cross network boundaries without proprietary constraints, making it easier to deploy APN services spanning multiple operators and enabling high-speed, energy-efficient communication across geographically distributed sites.

For network operators, the standard matters because it creates a technical baseline for interconnecting APNs operated by different providers. The framework specifically targets the requirements of AI workloads and physical AI applications, which need real-time processing of data from sensors and robots in remote locations. By standardizing how these high-capacity, low-latency optical paths are established between operators, it removes a barrier to global adoption that previously limited APN deployments to single administrative domains. The approval supports the Japanese Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications initiative to build future infrastructure for an AI-driven society under the Beyond 5G Promotion Strategy 2.0.

Research and development into photonic network federation technology continues, with work underway to develop and evaluate interconnection methods using actual equipment. The seven companies will continue standardization activities toward a further ITU-T recommendation on functional architecture for low-latency and energy-efficient networks, targeted for completion by October 2028. The IOWN Global Forum, which has been developing shared technical documents outlining APN architecture and functional requirements, is also contributing to this foundational work.
