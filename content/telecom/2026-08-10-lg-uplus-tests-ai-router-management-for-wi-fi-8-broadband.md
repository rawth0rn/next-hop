LG Uplus is testing AI-driven router management for Wi-Fi 8-compatible home broadband systems with Broadcom [Mobile World Live](https://www.mobileworldlive.com/operators/lg-uplus-broadcom-apply-ai-to-router-management/). The Korean carrier is applying machine learning to automate quality control in customer premises equipment. The approach moves beyond manual configuration and reactive troubleshooting.

The trial targets the broadband access edge, where carrier visibility traditionally stops at the modem. By embedding AI into router management, LG Uplus aims to control Wi-Fi performance parameters directly. Wi-Fi 8, the next generation of wireless LAN standards, will support higher densities and faster speeds, making automated optimization more valuable. The source does not specify which router models or AI techniques are under test, but the Broadcom partnership points toward chipset-level integration. This could involve real-time analysis of channel conditions, client behavior, and interference patterns to adjust settings automatically.

For network operators, residential Wi-Fi remains a blind spot and a major source of customer complaints. Automating quality control reduces the need for truck rolls and tier-one support calls. If AI can preempt issues by adapting to changing home environments, carriers improve subscriber satisfaction while cutting operational costs. The Wi-Fi 8 compatibility suggests LG Uplus is future-proofing its approach for standards still in development. Practitioners should note this is a test, not a live deployment, and the source provides no timeline or performance metrics. The Broadcom angle indicates hardware vendors are opening APIs for carrier-grade AI integration, which could become an industry pattern. The test nature means no commitments on hardware upgrades or subscriber impact are public yet.

The experiment shows carriers pushing AI from core networks into customer premises equipment. Success would mean scaling automated management across millions of home routers, a logistical challenge even if the AI proves effective. LG Uplus has not announced next steps, but the trial sets a benchmark for operator-led innovation at the network edge. Watch for follow-up announcements on trial results and vendor ecosystem support.
