SpaceX agreed to acquire 800 MHz spectrum licenses from Grain to build a terrestrial mobile network in the United States according to [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/networks/2026/10/09/spacex-to-buy-key-spectrum-that-could-help-starlink-mobile-become-major-us-cell-carrier/5302393). The purchase would let Starlink Mobile operate ground-based cell towers alongside its existing satellite constellation and potentially become a major carrier.

The deal covers spectrum in the 800 MHz band currently held by Grain. These frequencies propagate over long distances and penetrate buildings well. Network designers value such low-band spectrum because it requires fewer cell sites to cover rural areas compared to mid-band or millimeter wave alternatives. Incumbent carriers have long used similar bands for baseline nationwide coverage and indoor service.

Starlink Mobile currently provides direct-to-cell service from low Earth orbit satellites. Adding terrestrial infrastructure would create a hybrid network that hands off traffic between space and ground based on signal availability. This architecture targets indoor locations and urban canyons where satellite signals are blocked by structures. Traffic could route through the lowest-latency path available, switching automatically as users move between outdoor and indoor environments.

The move positions SpaceX to challenge incumbent United States carriers by offering a combined satellite and cellular service. Rather than relying solely on roaming agreements with existing operators, Starlink Mobile could operate its own ground infrastructure using the acquired licenses to become a major cell carrier. This changes the competitive landscape for rural coverage where incumbents have historically deployed minimal infrastructure and relied on roaming partnerships.

The acquisition requires regulatory approval. If completed, it would give Starlink Mobile control of spectrum assets needed to offer standalone cellular service instead of limited satellite-only coverage. Network planners should monitor the filing to understand which specific geographic markets the licenses cover and where new infrastructure might appear.
