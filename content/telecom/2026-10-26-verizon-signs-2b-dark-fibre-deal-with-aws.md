Verizon unveiled an agreement worth more than $2 billion with Amazon Web Services (AWS) to supply dark fibre infrastructure for AI workload demand, [Mobile World Live](https://www.mobileworldlive.com/verizon/verizon-bags-2b-aws-deal-hails-efficiency-drive/) reported.

Dark fibre is unlit optical fibre that a customer lights and manages with its own transmission equipment. Under this arrangement, AWS obtains dedicated physical strands rather than buying managed bandwidth services from Verizon. AWS will install its own optical gear, choose its own wavelengths, and control encryption and capacity planning across the routes. The model removes the service provider from the data plane and hands the cloud operator direct responsibility for the optical layer.

The structure reflects how hyperscale buyers increasingly prefer to own or directly control the physical path that connects data centers, cloud regions, and cluster sites. Lit services add a service wrapper and shared infrastructure that can introduce variability in latency, scheduling, and troubleshooting. Dark fibre removes that abstraction. It gives AWS the ability to scale optical capacity as needed without renegotiating transport contracts for each bandwidth increase.

For network practitioners, the deal illustrates a shift in procurement strategy. Cloud providers are moving from traditional wholesale transport agreements toward long-term physical infrastructure leases. The reported value, more than $2 billion, suggests a multi-year commitment that covers extensive metro and potentially intercity routes. For Verizon, the sale turns passive plant into a revenue stream while offloading the capital expense of lighting the fibre to AWS. For AWS, it secures predictable, physically isolated paths for the high-volume east-west traffic patterns that AI workloads generate.

This arrangement also highlights the tension between cloud operators and traditional carriers. Where carriers once sold managed circuits as a premium service, they now increasingly lease raw physical strands. Practitioners should expect more of these deals as AI clusters expand and demand dedicated, scalable fibre access.
