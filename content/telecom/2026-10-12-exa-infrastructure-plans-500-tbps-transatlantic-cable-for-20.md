EXA Infrastructure [announced](https://rcrwireless.com/20261008/ai-infrastructure/this-is-the-crunch-exa-builds-again-as-atlantic-capacity-runs-short) it will build a new 500 Tbps transatlantic subsea cable, the first wholesale system of its kind in over a decade. The 6,500 kilometer route, named Meridian, will run between New Jersey in the US and Brean in the UK, with a ready for service date in the fourth quarter of 2029.

The cable carries 24 fiber pairs and will deliver a half-petabit total capacity. EXA appointed Xtera as the turn-key provider for the build. The system addresses a capacity shortage that arrived earlier than forecast. EXA now expects its existing transatlantic capacity to exhaust around early 2027, nine months sooner than previous projections, with bridge capacity lasting only 12 to 18 months at current growth rates.

Hyperscalers including AWS, Google, and Meta are increasingly retaining capacity on their own cables for AI projects, removing expected supply from the wholesale market. Meanwhile, financial services firms and neo-clouds are buying significantly more bandwidth, shifting from 100G-class connections to full wavelengths. These customers now demand diverse routes for redundancy, with many requesting four separate paths where two previously sufficed. The supply chain for fiber-optic cables is tightening across land and sea routes, pushing EXA to commit now rather than wait until 2032 or 2033.

Meridian will carry traffic between AI factories in the US and enterprise premises in mainland Europe, including Frankfurt, Milan, and Istanbul. EXA has signed anchor customers for the route, covering what it describes as a double-digit percentage of the cable, though it declined to name specific tenants. The remaining capacity will target enterprises and neo-clouds rather than traditional telecommunications carriers, as EXA prefers customers who consume capacity directly rather than resell it.
