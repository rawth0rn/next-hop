Tampa Electric Company [selected](https://www.mobileworldlive.com/ericsson/us-utility-expands-private-lte-network/) Ericsson and OneLayer to deploy, manage, and secure its growing private Long Term Evolution (LTE) network. Burns & McDonnell will provide integration and engineering expertise for the utility's expansion across its service territory.

Private LTE gives utilities dedicated cellular infrastructure that operates independently of public carrier networks. Unlike Wi-Fi or unlicensed industrial radio, cellular Long Term Evolution provides wide-area coverage, mobility management, and predictable latency using spectrum the utility controls. For Tampa Electric, the deployment means field crews and grid devices connect over infrastructure the utility owns and operates directly, rather than riding on commercial macro networks that may have coverage gaps in rural service territories or during public emergencies.

OneLayer supplies the management and security platform, while Ericsson provides the radio access and core network equipment. Burns & McDonnell handles the systems integration work, connecting the cellular core to existing supervisory control and data acquisition systems. This three-vendor arrangement reflects how industrial private networks are now procured at scale: utilities buy integrated connectivity stacks with professional services built in, rather than assembling radio engineering teams from scratch.

For network operators in critical infrastructure, the shift matters for uptime and traffic segmentation. Public cellular networks prioritize consumer traffic and may deprioritize machine traffic during congestion. A private network keeps grid communications isolated from retail data storms and ensures availability when macro towers are overloaded. It also simplifies security policy enforcement when the utility manages Subscriber Identity Module authentication and the core user plane locally, rather than tunneling through a public operator's packet core and trusting external firewall rules. Incident response times improve when the network team controls the entire stack from radio to gateway.

The expansion indicates that private cellular is moving from pilot phases to operational status for North American utilities. As the footprint grows to cover more substations and distribution circuits, the operational burden shifts toward lifecycle management of the radio assets, software updates, and integration with legacy utility protocols.
