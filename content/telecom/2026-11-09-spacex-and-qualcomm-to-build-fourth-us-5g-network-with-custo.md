SpaceX announced it will build a terrestrial 5G network across the United States using custom silicon and Layer 1 software from Qualcomm, with deployment starting in 2027 [Light Reading](https://www.lightreading.com/5g/spacex-and-qualcomm-threaten-to-topple-us-market-s-cozy-5g-order).

The companies confirmed that Starlink Mobile will use Qualcomm's Dragonwing infrastructure platforms, including application-specific integrated circuits (ASICs) and commercial-grade Layer 1 software for radio access network (RAN) processing. The design relies on massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) configurations with up to 64 transmitters and receivers per radio unit, double the 32 typically deployed today. Qualcomm is engineering the technology to operate in SpaceX's 50MHz of 2GHz spectrum rather than the 3.5GHz band commonly used for massive MIMO. SpaceX stated it will develop the Layer 2 and Layer 3 software itself, likely running on third-party central processing units (CPUs), while potentially manufacturing its own radio units based on Qualcomm's designs or using contract manufacturers.

This matters for network operators because it revives the custom silicon approach at a time when incumbent vendors Nokia and Samsung have pivoted to general-purpose CPUs and graphics processing units (GPUs) for virtualized RAN. Qualcomm's ASICs offer higher efficiency for Layer 1 processing than general-purpose chips, though they lock operators into specific hardware and lack the flexibility to run other workloads. For practitioners, the entry of a fourth nationwide carrier could increase competition for spectrum auctions and pressure capital expenditure budgets if pricing wars erode margins. SpaceX's vertical integration, controlling both the silicon specification and upper-layer software stack, also bypasses the traditional vendor ecosystem of Ericsson, Nokia, and Samsung, potentially creating a new model for network buildouts while introducing supply chain risks unfamiliar to established operators.
