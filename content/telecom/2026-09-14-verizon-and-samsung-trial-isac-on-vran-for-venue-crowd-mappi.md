Verizon and Samsung turned live 5G radio signals into real-time crowd heatmaps during a large international soccer fan event, marking a field trial of 6G-ready Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) on commercial virtualised RAN (vRAN) [Mobile World Live](https://www.mobileworldlive.com/verizon/verizon-samsung-kick-off-venue-management-with-isac-trial/).

The trial used the existing 5G radio infrastructure to sense and map crowd density. ISAC repurposes communication signals as radar-like sensors, extracting environmental information from the radio frequency environment without deploying separate sensing hardware. Running on commercial vRAN gear means the capability rides on the same virtualized infrastructure that already handles 5G traffic, sharing compute and radio resources rather than requiring dedicated equipment.

For venue operators, the heatmaps provide live occupancy data. Stadium and arena managers can see crowd flow and density in real time, which helps with safety planning, staffing decisions, and concession logistics. The approach uses signals already in the air, avoiding the cost and installation of separate cameras or dedicated sensors across large facilities.

For network operators, the trial shows a path toward new services on existing infrastructure. Verizon can potentially offer venue analytics as a service without building a parallel sensing network. The vRAN architecture matters because it hosts the ISAC processing in software, making it possible to upgrade or scale the capability through software patches rather than hardware swaps. The 6G-ready label signals that standards work is ongoing, but the trial demonstrates early commercial feasibility on today’s gear.

The soccer event setting proves the concept at scale. Large venues with tens of thousands of attendees create complex radio environments. Successfully generating useful heatmaps in that context suggests the signal processing is robust enough for real-world deployment, not just lab conditions.

Verizon and Samsung have not detailed accuracy metrics, latency figures, or commercial launch timelines. The announcement focuses on the live demonstration rather than product availability. Operators watching this space should track 3GPP releases and ITU-R timelines for ISAC standardization, which will determine interoperability and spectrum rules.

Venue management becomes a concrete use case for a technology often discussed in abstract 6G research. For practitioners running large-scale networks, the key takeaway is that sensing capabilities may soon run as software features on the same vRAN platforms already being deployed for 5G capacity.
