Nokia Federal Solutions won a contract under the US Department of War’s Real-World Application Prototyping for ISAC Demonstration (RAPID) project to prototype integrated sensing and communications (ISAC) capabilities using 5G and 6G infrastructure for object detection and tracking. ([Mobile World Live](https://www.mobileworldlive.com/nokia/nokia-federal-secures-dow-isac-contract/))

The contract covers developing, building, and testing an end-to-end ISAC platform that repurposes cellular base stations as sensors. Nokia will build the system on its AI-RAN platform, combining multiple sensing methods with sensor fusion to create a unified view of unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) threats. The platform integrates third-party applications for sensing and spectrum threat awareness alongside Nokia’s software stack. Steve Vogelsang, Chief Technology Officer at Nokia Federal, noted that unmanned systems pose a real threat to critical infrastructure, and that using existing cellular networks for detection avoids the cost of standalone sensor deployments. Subcontractors include Texas Tech University, DeepSig, Hidden Level, and Future Technologies, plus Nokia affiliates.

For network operators and defense infrastructure managers, the project tests whether 5G and future 6G networks can replace dedicated radar installations for perimeter security. By using radio access network (RAN) equipment already deployed for communications, ISAC could reduce capital expenditure for wide-area surveillance while maintaining standard service. The approach also allows spectrum awareness to share infrastructure with traffic, potentially simplifying operations and maintenance. Pallavi Mahajan, chief technology and AI officer at Nokia, stated at TIP Fyuz 2026 that the company intends to open the platform for others to innovate on top, suggesting an ecosystem play rather than a closed proprietary system.

The work supports the FutureG Office mission to mature 6G capabilities ahead of standards debates expected at the 2027 World Radiocommunication Conference. The announcement follows Ericsson’s recent unveiling of its own ISAC system for 5G networks and its $100 million commitment to commercialize the technology within two years. The Department of War did not disclose the contract value or duration.
