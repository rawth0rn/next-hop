[Mobile World Live](https://www.mobileworldlive.com/nokia/optus-nokia-detail-upper-6ghz-test-outcomes/) reports Optus and Nokia completed upper 6GHz tests in Australia, work the operator says could prove fundamental for a future move to 6G.

The trial puts Optus among a growing group of carriers exploring the upper 6GHz band for eventual mobile service. While the companies released no technical details, such tests typically measure propagation, interference, and equipment performance in spectrum that regulators have not yet allocated for cellular use. The band sits above the portion already assigned to unlicensed Wi-Fi in many markets, making it a candidate for licensed mobile service.

For 6G, wide contiguous channels matter. Upper 6GHz could offer the hundreds of megahertz needed for throughput targets, with better range than millimeter wave. That makes it a candidate for the next generation of mid-band deployments. The band's physical characteristics are closer to 3.5 GHz than to 28 GHz, so it reuses many existing mid-band techniques while delivering more capacity.

Spectrum decisions drive network architecture. If upper 6GHz wins mobile allocation, carriers will need new radio hardware, antenna designs, and planning tools. The tests also feed regulatory filings at bodies like the International Telecommunication Union, where allocations take years to settle. For practitioners, early trials signal where vendors and operators are placing bets, which shapes interoperability work and upgrade timelines.

The timing is notable. 6G standards work is just beginning, with the first drafts expected around 2026 and commercial deployment targeted for 2030. Spectrum identified now gets written into channel models and radio specifications early, making it harder to change later. Operators who test today influence which bands become de facto standards.

For Optus, the work aligns with a broader industry push. Regulators in Europe and Asia are also studying upper 6GHz, and equipment vendors are building radios that can tune the band. Network planners should monitor these trials because they set the foundation for future capex cycles. If upper 6GHz becomes a 6G core band, today's multi-band antennas may need replacement, and propagation databases will require updates.

The tests add a data point on the long runway to 6G. Network teams should watch which bands gain traction, as field tests today shape policy and standards tomorrow.
