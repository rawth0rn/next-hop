Ciena, Toshiba, and Quantum Corridor completed a 1.6 Tb/s quantum-safe optical encryption trial on a live commercial network between Chicago and Hammond, Indiana. The test combined National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)-certified post-quantum cryptography (PQC) with quantum key distribution (QKD) to protect data in transit, according to the [announcement](https://www.fierce-network.com/modernization/ciena-toshiba-complete-live-quantum-networking-trial-live-midwest-network).

The trial ran on Ciena's Waveserver platform powered by WaveLogic 6 Extreme (WL6e) while simultaneously supporting existing WaveLogic 5 Extreme encrypted traffic on the same network. Ciena's WL6e enables a hybrid security approach that runs NIST-certified PQC algorithms and interoperates with Toshiba's QKD servers, which generate quantum-based encryption keys. The Quantum Corridor network spans the Illinois-Indiana state line, connecting the Chicago ORD10 data center at 350 East Cermak to the Digital Crossroad campus in Hammond. Ciena has worked with Quantum Corridor for several years, including previous quantum networking research involving JPMorgan Chase and Toshiba.

For network operators, the test shows a practical upgrade path. Existing Ciena customers with 800-gig encryption can adopt PQC protections through software updates without replacing hardware or disrupting live traffic. The approach directly addresses "harvest now, decrypt later" attacks, where adversaries store encrypted data today to decrypt later with quantum computers. The trial also demonstrated that PQC and QKD can coexist with legacy services on the same infrastructure, which matters for operators managing service continuity.

As governments accelerate quantum-readiness mandates, operators can implement NIST-compliant PQC now and evaluate QKD as an additional physical layer. Paulina Gomez, director of product marketing for Ciena, noted that expectations for Q-Day continue to move closer. "Every time the industry looks at it or you take a pulse on it, it seems like the window is shortening," she said. The trial signals that quantum-safe upgrades can happen on production networks at multi-terabit speeds without a forklift replacement.
