Viasat and Space42 [committed $1 billion in combined equity](https://www.lightreading.com/satellite/viasat-space42-fire-up-equatys-jv-for-d2d) to establish Equatys, a joint venture that will operate shared satellite and ground infrastructure for Direct-to-Device (D2D) mobile services.

Equatys will function as a wholesale infrastructure provider, similar to a TowerCo model but for space-based connectivity. The platform connects standard smartphones and Internet of Things (IoT) devices directly to satellites using Third Generation Partnership Project (3GPP) Non-Terrestrial Network (NTN) standards, eliminating the need for specialized satellite handsets. Viasat will serve as the prime technology contractor, building on existing technical collaboration between the two companies on satellite design and manufacture. The initial constellation development accelerates under stepped-up joint funding agreements signed simultaneously with the formation documents.

The venture combines access to more than 100 MHz of globally coordinated Mobile Satellite Service (MSS) spectrum with commercial relationships spanning over 400 mobile operators worldwide. This addresses coverage gaps in remote areas and capacity constraints in populated metro regions where terrestrial networks lack density or availability. The shared infrastructure supports voice, text, and data services where cellular networks cannot reach.

For mobile network operators, Equatys offers a capital-efficient path to extend coverage without the expense of building dedicated satellite constellations. Instead of each operator negotiating separate infrastructure deals or managing proprietary satellite integrations, they can purchase wholesale capacity from the shared platform. This lowers the barrier to offering satellite fallback services for areas outside cellular range. The model also standardizes integration with existing mobile cores through 3GPP NTN protocols, reducing interoperability complexity compared to bespoke satellite systems. Mobile operators gain a vendor-neutral option for filling coverage holes while avoiding the operational overhead of maintaining their own space segments.

The companies announced the agreement at World Space Business Week in Paris, advancing plans first disclosed in March 2025.
