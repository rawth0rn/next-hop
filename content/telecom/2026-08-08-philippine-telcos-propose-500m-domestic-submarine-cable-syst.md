PLDT, Globe Telecom, and Converge ICT Solutions have submitted a proposal to the Philippine Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) for a $500 million nationwide submarine cable system that would span the country's major island groups [Light Reading](https://www.lightreading.com/cable-technology/philippine-telcos-pool-resources-for-500m-domestic-subsea-cable-system). The three carriers formed a consortium to install new domestic fiber rather than lease capacity on existing international systems, representing a significant capital commitment to national infrastructure.

The proposed route forms a loop from Batanes to Palawan and the Visayas, then to Mindanao, returning to Batanes via the Pacific Ocean. The system would include landing stations connected to 14 international submarine cables, effectively creating a domestic ring that links to global networks. Henry Aguda, DICT minister, stated that construction could begin this year and reach completion within 24 months of government approval, with a target operational date before June 2028.

The consortium plans to integrate acoustic sensing technology into the fiber cables. This would allow the network to function as a continuous sensing array for detecting seismic activity, tsunamis, and vessel movements within Philippine territorial waters. Aguda explained that the technology transforms the cables into a monitoring asset similar to a spider web, where movement indicates activity. Six government agencies will participate in the project, including the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) and the Department of Science and Technology (DOST) through the Philippine Institute of Volcanology and Seismology (Phivolcs) for disaster warning, the Department of National Defense (DND) for maritime security, and the Department of Energy (DOE) since power lines can be laid alongside the cable.

The project complements the government-backed National Fiber Backbone Project, which installs terrestrial fiber to connect government offices and schools. While the terrestrial project handles local access, the submarine system provides national backbone capacity and physical redundancy across the archipelago.

For network operators, the build represents a shift from reliance on international cable consortiums to a domestically controlled infrastructure asset. The integrated sensing capability also creates a dual-use model where commercial fiber assets serve public safety and defense functions. This arrangement potentially reduces the need for standalone government monitoring systems while providing additional revenue justification for the private investment. The involvement of multiple agencies indicates the cable will carry both commercial traffic and real-time sensor data for national security and disaster response applications. Landing stations will need to support both standard optical transport equipment and specialized monitoring systems for the acoustic sensors.
