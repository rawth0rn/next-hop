AT&T has taken [OTel 2.0](https://rcrwireless.com/20260828/ai/att-otel-2-0-production) into full production deployment, running the open-source telecom AI model on AMD Instinct GPUs and Dell servers rather than the CUDA-centric stacks that dominate most AI infrastructure today.

The model is a 31 billion parameter specialist built on Google's Gemma 4 31B-IT base and post-trained for telecom workloads. AT&T and its partners processed over a trillion tokens during training, distilling that pool down to roughly 400 billion highly relevant telecom tokens for post-training. That corpus includes a 15 billion token curated dataset and a 10 billion token Telco Corpus from GSMA, drawing on materials from 3GPP, ETSI, CAMARA, ITU, O-RAN, and TM Forum, augmented with synthetic data generated by open models like Phi-4. Training ran on roughly 430 AMD Instinct MI300X GPUs via Microsoft Foundry on Azure, while Dell supplies carrier-grade servers hosting AMD MI355X GPUs for on-premises deployment.

OTel 2.0 sits behind an AI Gateway that caches responses and routes tasks between the domain model, other specialists, and frontier models depending on complexity. The live infrastructure processes around 45 billion tokens per day across more than 100 generative AI models, with about 40 percent of those operations running on AMD hardware. AT&T says this routing and caching cuts inference costs by up to 90 percent compared to using frontier models for everything, though replicating those savings requires the scale and engineering resources to implement complex caching setups.

For network operators, the deployment offers a path to run telecom-tuned AI on right-sized hardware in their own data centers rather than relying exclusively on hyperscale clusters. The architecture reduces dependence on closed AI and single GPU vendors, though it shifts dependency to specific cloud services and open-source infrastructure stacks. Security remains a concern, as dropping open models into mission-critical infrastructure introduces new attack surfaces including prompt injection and model poisoning. Model weights and documentation are publicly available on Hugging Face with instructions for reproducible evaluation.
