Vocus has appointed UGL as the development partner for Australia's first ducted intercapital fiber network between Sydney and Melbourne, a project costing approximately A$500 million (US$358.19 million) [Light Reading](https://www.lightreading.com/cable-technology/vocus-taps-ugl-to-develop-sydney-melbourne-fiber-route).

The route is the first project under Vocus' Australian Digital Infrastructure Platform (ADIP), a multi-year initiative addressing Australia's fiber capacity gap during the AI boom. ADIP aims to expand subsea, intercapital, and metro fiber infrastructure to support economic growth and digital transformation. Vocus chief technology officer Nikos Katinakis said the company selected UGL for its long track record delivering Australian digital infrastructure. UGL will manage early works including surveys, design, engineering, field validation, stakeholder engagement, project management, and cost modeling to prepare the project for construction. Katinakis called the selection "the start of detailed route design and readiness activities on this route."

The ducted design breaks from Australia's traditional direct-burial long-haul fiber. In direct burial, fragile glass fibers sit in the ground. The ducted approach places fibers inside protective pipes, letting operators add capacity by blowing additional fiber pairs through existing ducts. This avoids digging up ground or disrupting active customer lines. Katinakis noted the approach provides "greater resilience and protection against cable cuts." The Sydney-Melbourne route can accommodate up to 6,912 fiber cores, or about 3,456 fiber pairs.

For network operators, this changes the capacity expansion model. The Sydney-Melbourne corridor currently carries roughly 40% of Australia's long-haul data traffic. Vocus estimates that without new infrastructure, the corridor will soon be undersupplied. By 2030, the company forecasts Australian data center capacity will triple, with AI workloads driving 85% to 95% of long-haul fiber demand and 70% to 80% of metro demand. Ducted infrastructure lets providers scale to meet that growth without construction crews or service outages. The technology is standard among leading AI and cloud providers in North America and Europe but has not previously been used on a long-haul Australian route.

Construction is underway. The route is expected to be operational by 2029.
