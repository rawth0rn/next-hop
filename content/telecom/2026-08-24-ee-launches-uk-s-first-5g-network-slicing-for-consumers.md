EE has launched the UK’s first commercial 5G network slicing offer for consumers and small businesses. [Light Reading](https://www.lightreading.com/5g/ee-joins-europe-s-growing-5g-slicing-cohort-with-consumer-offer) reports the service, called Fast Lane, is available for an additional £5 per month with the EE Full Works Plus plan. Small enterprises can also sign up through the EE Full Works business plan.

The service runs on EE’s 5G Standalone (SA) network, branded 5G+. It anticipates network congestion and moves customer connections into a dedicated slice during busy periods. EE trialed the technology for two years at venues including Wembley Stadium, the Belfast Christmas Market, Sail GP in Portsmouth, the BAFTAs, and the Royal Welsh Show 2026. The trials focused on maintaining connectivity under heavy load.

EE joins a growing cohort of European operators deploying commercial slicing. The June 2026 Ericsson Mobility Report counted 84 commercially available slicing offers globally out of 151 total, up from 65 six months prior. European operators account for 34 or 35 of those offers. Recent regional launches include Vodafone Business’s 5G+ Local Slicing for enterprises in April, Proximus’s three-slice deployment for a soccer match in Belgium where one slice served medical assistance, another media coverage, and a third mobile payments, and WindTre’s SME service in Italy that creates virtual private networks for business-critical applications. In 2025, Elisa in Finland, Orange France, and OTE in Greece also launched services. Orange has been particularly active, offering 5G+ Home fixed wireless access for residential customers and dedicated bandwidth for SMEs.

The move marks a shift from trials to mainstream commercialization, but the report classifies EE’s offer as a generic “boost” service rather than a targeted solution. Ericsson notes such broad offerings often fail to address specific consumer needs and risk becoming “just another G.” More effective approaches focus on gaming, live event connectivity, video calling, or premium fixed wireless access. EE’s own trial at the Belfast Christmas Market in 2024 used slicing to keep payment terminals online, a concrete use case the commercial service does not replicate. The report also warns that operators must align marketing strategies to scale these services, citing evidence that app-integrated offers are up to 20 times more effective than other channels. About half of the 14 new trials and proofs of concept are exploring use cases beyond current commercial offerings, including self-driving trucks, autonomous drones, and advanced public safety applications.

As 5G SA networks approach 95 deployments globally, more operators can launch slicing, but translating capability into tangible benefits remains the challenge. Technology alone is not enough without clear customer value.
