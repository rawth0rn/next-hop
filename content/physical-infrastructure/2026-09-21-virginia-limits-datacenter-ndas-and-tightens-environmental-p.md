Virginia governor issued an [executive order](https://www.theregister.com/offbeat/2026/09/21/nasa-gives-spacex-a-billion-reasons-to-keep-flying-crew-dragon/5297705) that puts the kibosh on non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) for datacenter projects, limits permitting, and calls for stiffer environmental protections. The move addresses growing political opposition to datacenter expansion in the state, where rapid construction of cloud computing facilities has strained local power grids and water resources.

The order prohibits confidential agreements between developers and state agencies that previously kept project details from public disclosure. It also restricts new permitting approvals while establishing additional environmental review requirements for facility construction. The directive specifically targets the transparency of arrangements between operators and government entities, requiring public access to contracts and planning documents that were previously sealed from competitors and community groups.

For network operators, the policy change removes a tool commonly used to shield commercial negotiations and project specifications. The new transparency requirements apply to existing projects currently under NDA as well as future developments. The environmental provisions add a review layer that will likely extend deployment timelines for new builds and require additional documentation of power and water consumption. Operators may face revised standards for cooling efficiency and carbon emissions before receiving construction approvals. Compliance costs will likely increase for new facility deployments in the region.

The order signals that Virginia now treats datacenter expansion as critical infrastructure requiring public oversight and environmental scrutiny rather than purely economic development. The state will operate under these interim restrictions while developing permanent regulations through a formal review process.

Operators should prepare for longer approval cycles and public disclosure of project details previously considered proprietary. The shift marks a significant change in how the state balances cloud computing growth against local resource concerns and political pressure from residential communities.
