Teravolt plans to convert legacy industrial sites, including former Bitcoin farms and aluminum smelters, into data centers to meet artificial intelligence power demand, [The Register reports](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/09/14/teravolt-looks-to-cannibalize-older-industries-to-meet-ai-power-demand/5296014).

Bitcoin mining operations and aluminum smelting facilities already draw massive amounts of electricity. They include high-capacity power feeds, substations, and cooling infrastructure. Aluminum smelters require continuous power supplies and heavy-duty electrical infrastructure. Bitcoin farms feature dense layouts and power distribution designed for constant computational loads. Both facility types include industrial-grade cooling systems designed to dissipate heat from power-hungry equipment.

By repurposing these existing installations, Teravolt can potentially bypass aspects of data center construction. The physical shells and security perimeters remain intact. The sites maintain established grid interconnections with utilities that sized transformers and transmission lines for heavy industrial loads. This eliminates the utility upgrade cycles that often stretch across multiple seasons and delay new hyperscale facilities.

For network operators and infrastructure teams, this approach offers a faster path to adding compute capacity where the power already exists. Aluminum smelters in particular require connections and cooling systems that align with the density demands of AI training clusters. Bitcoin farms already have power distribution and cooling layouts suited for high-density racks, often with excess capacity remaining after mining economics turned unfavorable. The existing electrical infrastructure can often support modern equipment with minimal retrofitting.

The strategy matters because power availability, not fiber or land, now gates AI cluster deployment in many regions. Reusing industrial brownfield sites cuts the timeline from years to months and avoids the regulatory and grid interconnection queues that delay new builds. It also puts stranded assets back into productive use without requiring new environmental permits or greenfield construction. For teams racing to bring AI capacity online, the ability to inherit megawatt-scale infrastructure represents a practical way to bypass the current supply constraints.
