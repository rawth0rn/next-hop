Telegeography delivered a conference presentation titled "Global Internet infrastructure" at the North American Network Operators Group (NANOG) 98 meeting in mid-October 2026 ([Global Internet infrastructure at NANOG 98](https://blog.apnic.net/2026/10/28/global-internet-infrastructure-at-nanog-98/)). The keynote documented a structural shift in how global backbone capacity is funded and operated. Hyperscalers including Google, Meta, Microsoft, and Amazon now cover 38 percent of new cable investments as private systems. They also fund 25 percent of consortium projects. The transatlantic route reflects this trend most sharply, with 85 percent of funding coming from private hyperscaler investments.

This ownership model replaces the traditional consortium lease structure. The presentation notes that market concentration allows these four companies to accumulate large capital reserves. They can now build private fibre routes to control delivery without third-party dependencies. A single dominant lessee controlling a consortium creates insolvency risk if that company exits the agreement. Private ownership removes that financial exposure. Unit costs for capacity are falling as new systems use two-core fibre strands and expanded optical bands. These technical improvements support larger capacity increments, which now reach 2 Pbps annually and will exceed 10 Pbps by 2035.

Network operators need to adjust procurement and resilience planning around this shift. The annual investment in new cable systems sits between USD 5B and USD 6B through 2029. Operators who rely on standard consortium capacity may face reduced availability on high-traffic routes. They will need to negotiate directly with hyperscaler-backed platforms or diversify across secondary regions. The move also concentrates maintenance control. Foreign private entities now manage significant portions of the repair vessel fleet and maintenance zones. Practitioners should track which routes carry hyperscaler-owned capacity and verify their own fallback options before deploying critical workloads.
