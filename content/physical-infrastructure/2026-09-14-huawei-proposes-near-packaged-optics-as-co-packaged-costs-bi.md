Huawei is pitching near-packaged optics as a cost-conscious alternative to co-packaged optics for AI clusters, according to a report in [The Register Networks](https://www.theregister.com/networks/2026/09/14/huawei-pitches-near-packaged-optics-as-co-packaged-costs-bite/5296164). The company presented the approach in a recent briefing, highlighting mounting cost pressures as a barrier to co-packaged optics adoption.

Co-packaged optics integrate optical engines directly with switch chips, promising lower power and latency for high-speed interconnects. But manufacturing complexity has pushed costs higher than many data center operators can justify. Huawei's near-packaged design places optics millimeters from the switch die rather than directly on the package, delivering most electrical benefits without the premium.

The design keeps optical modules extremely close to the switch ASIC, using short electrical traces to minimize signal loss. Engineers can maintain high switch throughput while using standard optical components that benefit from existing supply chains and testing infrastructure. The approach also reduces thermal management challenges compared to fully co-packaged solutions.

For network operators building AI training clusters, the cost difference matters. Huawei positioned near-packaged optics as a middle tier between traditional pluggable transceivers and premium co-packaged designs. This gives operators a way to preserve compact form factors in dense racks without absorbing the full cost increase of co-packaged integration.

The timing reflects broader market hesitation. Major cloud providers have reportedly delayed co-packaged optics rollouts. Huawei's pitch offers switch vendors and operators a less risky path that can ship sooner.

Practitioners evaluating next-generation AI fabrics must weigh three options: traditional pluggables, co-packaged optics, and near-packaged designs. The choice hinges on rack density targets, budget cycles, and tolerance for integration risk. Near-packaged optics may win where space is tight but cost control remains critical.
