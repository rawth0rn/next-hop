Onomondo [launched](https://iot-now.com/2026/09/17/158452-onomondo-sgp-32-esim-iot-remote-manager-eim/) its own eSIM IoT Remote Manager (eIM), a remote control for cellular profiles that is designed to prevent the management layer itself from becoming a new vendor lock-in point under the GSMA’s SGP.32 standard.

SGP.32 defines three interoperable components for IoT eSIM: the eUICC (embedded Universal Integrated Circuit Card) chip, downloadable eSIM profiles, and the eIM that orchestrates them. The standard mandates that the eUICC must support remote reconfiguration, but it makes the same operations optional for the eIM. If an eIM vendor keeps its configuration interfaces closed, the customer cannot replace the eIM without physically accessing the device. That creates a de facto lock-in, leaving enterprises dependent on the original provider’s ecosystem even when the hardware supports switching.

Onomondo built its eIM to be fully configurable and independent of its own connectivity. Customers can use it to download, enable, disable, and delete profiles on devices regardless of which carrier provides the cellular service. The company also sells the complete stack, including eUICC SIMs and global connectivity, but the eIM works with third-party profiles and networks.

For fleets numbering in the hundreds of thousands or millions, with operational lifetimes of ten years or more, the practical ability to change providers matters more than theoretical portability. If the eIM acts as a closed gateway, switching carriers later requires retrieving devices from vehicles, industrial machinery, or meters distributed worldwide. That operational burden recreates the exact problem remote SIM provisioning was meant to eliminate. By keeping the eIM open and interchangeable, Onomondo preserves the "freedom to leave" that SGP.32 promised without forcing a physical swap of the SIM estate.

The eIM is available today, either as a standalone component or as part of Onomondo’s complete SGP.32 ecosystem.
