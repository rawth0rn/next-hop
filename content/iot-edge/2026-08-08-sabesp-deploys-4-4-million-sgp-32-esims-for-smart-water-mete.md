SABESP, Vivo, and IDEMIA Secure Transactions have launched the infrastructure to connect 4.4 million smart water meters across São Paulo using embedded SIM technology compliant with the GSMA SGP.32 standard ([IoT Now](https://iot-now.com/2026/08/05/157777-sabesp-partners-with-vivo-and-idemia-secure-transactions/)). The 14-year deployment covers São Paulo City and São José dos Campos, representing one of the largest operational Internet of Things fleets in the utility sector.

The project relies on IDEMIA Secure Transactions' eSIMs and eSIM IoT platform rather than traditional removable SIM cards. GSMA SGP.32 specifies the remote provisioning architecture for machine-to-machine and Internet of Things devices, allowing operators to change connectivity profiles over the air without physical access to the meter. This capability matters for water infrastructure, where meters remain in service for over a decade and may need to switch networks or update cryptographic certificates during their lifecycle. SGP.32 replaces earlier standards that required more complex integration between device manufacturers and mobile operators.

SABESP selected the technology to support hourly consumption monitoring and leak detection across its distribution network. The deployment includes cryptographic agility features intended to address post-quantum security requirements, though specific migration timelines remain unspecified in the announcement. Vivo provides the cellular connectivity backbone, while IDEMIA manages the secure element provisioning and subscription management for the 4.4 million endpoints.

For network operators and utility engineers, the deployment demonstrates operational scale for SGP.32 implementations. Managing millions of endpoints with remote provisioning capabilities eliminates truck rolls for carrier changes, but it also concentrates risk in the subscription management servers that handle profile downloads. The 14-year contract length implies long-term interoperability commitments between the utility, mobile operator, and eSIM vendor that will outlast several hardware refresh cycles.

The meters will transmit consumption data to help SABESP identify post-meter leaks and optimize water distribution. By using standards-compliant eSIM infrastructure rather than proprietary connectivity modules, the utility gains flexibility to change network providers without replacing hardware, a consideration that becomes relevant as Brazil's cellular networks evolve and older radio technologies sunset. This approach sets a precedent for other utilities considering massive scale Internet of Things deployments with multi-decade service lifetimes.
