[ABB and Samsung Electronics](https://iot-now.com/2026/11/12/159322-abb-samsung-integrated-iot-solution-commercial-buildings/) launched an integrated Internet of Things (IoT) solution for commercial buildings that allows trained system integrators to deliver both companies' automation, energy management, and display technologies through a single provider.

The joint offering combines ABB's building automation and energy management portfolio with Samsung's smart building platforms. Participating integrators receive training on both ecosystems and can design, install, and configure complete packaged solutions covering lighting, climate control, shading, access control, energy metering, heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC) systems, and digital displays. Customers select either ABB Ability BuildingPro or Samsung SmartThings Pro as their primary management platform. The two platforms communicate through a secure cloud-to-cloud integration that exchanges building data and provides unified visibility into connected systems, energy performance, and operational insights. The combined offering also incorporates ABB-free@home for smart building automation, ABB InSite for energy monitoring, and the full ABB KNX portfolio of room-level devices and sensors.

Commercial building projects traditionally fragment across multiple suppliers, each with separate systems, contracts, and support channels. This fragmentation increases cost, extends timelines, and adds risk while obscuring post-deployment performance visibility. The integrated channel lets building owners and investors work with one trained integrator rather than coordinating disparate vendors. The approach arrives as regulatory pressure mounts in Europe. The revised Energy Performance of Buildings Directive will extend building automation and digitalization requirements to smaller non-residential buildings by the end of 2029, expanding the addressable market for unified automation platforms.

The solution targets offices, retail stores, restaurant chains, hotels, and apartment buildings through the growing network of dual-trained system integrators.
