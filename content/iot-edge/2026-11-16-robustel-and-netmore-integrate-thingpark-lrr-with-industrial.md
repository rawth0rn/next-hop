Robustel and Netmore [announced a technology partnership](https://iot-now.com/2026/11/10/159293-robustel-netmore-strategic-partnership-global-lorawan-deployments/) to integrate Netmore's ThingPark Long Range Relay (LRR) and Semtech LoRa Basics Station into Robustel's industrial gateway portfolio. The collaboration validates ThingPark LRR on Robustel hardware beginning with the R1520LG, while adding standards-based provisioning through LoRa Basics Station with CUPS.

The integration gives ThingPark customers two distinct onboarding paths. ThingPark LRR provides native compatibility with Netmore's network architecture, establishing the technical foundation for LRR support across Robustel's future LoRaWAN gateways. Parallel support for LoRa Basics Station with CUPS (Configuration and Update Server) offers secure, standards-based gateway provisioning and packet forwarding for rapid deployment without requiring deep platform integration.

The partnership addresses the shifting requirements of industrial LoRaWAN as networks expand into utilities, buildings, and operational technology environments. Robustel's LG3120e illustrates this transition, combining LoRaWAN connectivity, 4G/LTE backhaul, eSIM support, and a Linux-based edge computing environment. Through Robustel E2C Field integration, the gateway processes data from industrial equipment using Modbus, OPC UA, and BACnet alongside LoRaWAN device traffic. This allows the gateway to function as part of the application architecture rather than serving only as connectivity infrastructure between sensors and the cloud.

For practitioners, the arrangement creates practical options in both directions. Existing ThingPark customers gain access to Robustel's industrial gateway family, from compact packet forwarders to edge-computing platforms, supported by distribution channels in over 100 countries. Organizations currently operating self-managed LoRaWAN infrastructure on Robustel hardware can migrate to Netmore's professionally operated ThingPark platform without replacing gateways or changing hardware strategy. Robustel gateway variants support major LoRaWAN regional frequency plans used across international markets.

This dual pathway simplifies multi-territory deployments. Network operators can standardize on a common hardware family while adapting gateway capabilities to specific site requirements, whether simple metering or complex OT integration. The combination of global technology availability and local support channels addresses the operational realities of expanding LoRaWAN networks into core infrastructure across Europe, the Americas, and Asia-Pacific.
