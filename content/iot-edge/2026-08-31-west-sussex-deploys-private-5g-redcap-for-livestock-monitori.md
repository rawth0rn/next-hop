West Sussex County Council has connected environmental sensors from agritech specialist Pneumonitor to a [private 5G network](https://iot-now.com/2026/08/31/158136-west-sussex-advances-smart-livestock-monitoring-with-pneumonitor-and-boldyn-networks-private-5g/) operated by Boldyn Networks, adding livestock building monitoring to the Growing Sussex connected-agriculture programme.

The deployment uses 5G Reduced Capability (RedCap) to link Pneumonitor's in-pen devices, which measure temperature, humidity and other conditions where animals are located rather than at wall-mounted points. RedCap provides a trimmed-down 5G connection that trades bandwidth for lower power consumption and extended battery life. For sensors distributed across agricultural buildings, where power outlets are scarce and maintenance trips are costly, these traits determine whether a deployment is economically viable at scale. Unlike standard 5G, which would drain batteries and require frequent replacement, RedCap keeps hardware in place longer while still delivering the latency and reliability needed for timely environmental alerts.

For network operators and systems integrators, the installation demonstrates how private cellular can support IoT scale in environments where Wi-Fi coverage gaps and cabling constraints limit traditional deployments. The Growing Sussex programme, established by West Sussex County Council to trial 5G, IoT and AI in food production, went live with four private 5G networks by December 2025. The Pneumonitor integration now layers environmental monitoring atop that foundation, extending the network from fixed infrastructure to mobile animal husbandry contexts.

The operational benefit is straightforward. Farmers receive near real-time data on heat load and air quality inside pens, allowing intervention before respiratory stress or yield loss occurs. Because RedCap reduces device power requirements compared to standard 5G, the sensors can run longer between service intervals and deploy to more sites without infrastructure overhauls. Boldyn Networks and West Sussex County Council announced the four-network plan in 2024. The livestock monitoring component shows the architecture expanding from connectivity backbone to active farm management tool, with implications for how rural coverage models are built elsewhere.
